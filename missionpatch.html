Visual Translation Game

The initial visual translation game warm-up[cite: 6]. We began by asking what message Baruch would send into space, generating rapid icon associations around core motifs like curiosity, discovery, handshakes, teamwork, magnify glasses, and weightlessness[cite: 6].

Extracted Prompts & Ideas

02

Space Research Mind Map

Expanding on space exploration concepts through a structured mind map[cite: 7]. Focus centered on scientific observation, astronomy, lab equipment, orbit dynamics, high-tech machinery, and the crew of workers driving space station research[cite: 7].

Key Elements
Astronomy Space Station Goggles & Lab Coats Orbit & Satellites Microscopes
03

Baruch Community Mind Map

Grounding the space concept in the identity of our campus community[cite: 8]. Exploring student life around Lexington Campus, Flatiron district, multicultural diversity, urban bustle, and collaborative spirit alongside quirky campus realities like broken elevators[cite: 8].

Campus Identifiers
Lexington Campus Multicultural Flatiron District Collaborative Urban & Packed
04

Rapid Concept Frameworks

Rapid concept generation exploring six distinct visual avenues: Geometric, Narrative, Abstract, Symbolic, Minimal, and Unexpected[cite: 9]. Developing quick graphical shapes and visual layouts corresponding to each strategic direction[cite: 9].

Explored Frameworks
Geometric Narrative Abstract Symbolic Minimal ★ Unexpected
05

Final Patch Sketch & Chosen Direction

The final mission patch composite sketch[cite: 10]. I chose the Unexpected approach as my core design direction, synthesising the prominent Baruch banner typography, celestial orbits, number badges, and geometric patch frame into a cohesive emblem[cite: 10].
